## Task 16 -- Simple Profit Analysis

## Overview

This project was completed as part of the Veda Technology Data
Analytics Internship -- Task 16.

The objective of this task is to compare profit across different product
categories and understand basic profitability using Microsoft Excel.

## Objective

Compare total profit across product categories.

Compare average profit across product categories.

Understand basic profitability.

Present the results using tables and charts.

## Analysis Performed

The following analysis was performed in Excel:

Grouped records by product category.

Calculated Total Profit for each category.

Calculated Average Profit for each category.

Created a profit summary table.

Created a Total Profit by Product Category column chart.

Created an Average Profit by Product Category column chart.

Added key observations from the analysis.

## Key Findings

Technology generated the highest total profit of ₹10,254.24.

Furniture generated ₹2,918.80 in total profit.

Office Supplies generated the lowest total profit of ₹1,106.21.

Technology also had the highest average profit of ₹683.62 per
record.

The total profit shown in the summary is ₹14,279.25.

The category comparison provides a simple view of profitability
differences.

## Business Interpretation

The analysis shows that profitability varies across product categories.
Technology contributes the largest amount of profit in the provided
summary and also has the highest average profit per record. Office
Supplies contributes the lowest profit among the three categories.

This type of analysis can help businesses review category performance,
pricing, costs, discounts, and product mix.

## Conclusion

The Simple Profit Analysis successfully compares total and average
profit across product categories using Excel. The summary table and
charts make it easier to understand differences in profitability and
provide a basic foundation for business decision-making.

## Interview Questions

1. What is the difference between sales and profit?

Sales are the revenue generated from selling products, while profit is
the amount remaining after deducting relevant costs and expenses.

2. Why can sales be high but profit low?

Sales can be high while profit is low because of high costs, discounts,
operating expenses, or products with low profit margins.
